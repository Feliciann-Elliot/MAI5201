## Citation
Brown, T. B., Mann, B., Ryder, N., Subbiah, M., Kaplan, J., Dhariwal, P., ... & Amodei, D. (2020). Language models are few-shot learners. *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems*, 33, 1877-1901.

## Summary
This 60-page paper "Language Models are Few-Shot Learners" introduces the famous 175,000,000,000 parameter GPT-3 model. It shows that scaling the model improves the few shot-learning significantly which allowed it to produce superior performance in translation, answering questions and arithmetic – without fine tuning! The model outperforms existing high performing models trained on large datasets. According to the paper, limitations include inconsistent performance across datasets and biases resulting from training data. This model can generate human-like text which raises concerns about negative societal impacts such as misinformation and unethical usage

### What is most interesting in the paper?
The huge impact of model scaling to performance is quite interesting. The approach simplifies training and enables LLMs to generalize across diverse datasets using pure natural language

### What could the paper have done better?
There was a lack of in-depth explanation about how exactly the scaling led to better performance. 

### What questions do you have from reading the paper?
How does in-context learning in GPT-3 scale with model size, and what are the theoretical limits of this approach? ```
